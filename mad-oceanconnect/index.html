Understanding resistance codes

HR (high resistance) means a variety strongly restricts the development of the named pest or disease under normal pressure. IR (intermediate resistance) means it restricts development, but may show more symptoms than a high-resistance variety, especially under heavy pressure. Resistance is not immunity. Performance, yield and disease pressure vary with local conditions, growing system and crop management.

CodeDisease or pestType
Immunity

The plant is not attacked or infected by a given pest or pathogen.

Resistance

The variety limits the growth of a given pest or pathogen, or the damage it causes, compared with susceptible varieties in the same conditions. Under heavy pressure some symptoms can still appear.

High resistance (HR)

Strongly limits the pest or pathogen under normal pressure, compared with susceptible varieties.

Intermediate resistance (IR)

Limits the pest or pathogen, but can show more symptoms or damage than a highly resistant variety.

Definitions follow the International Seed Federation (ISF). Resistance does not cover new strains or races of a pathogen.

Common questions

What information do I need to get started?

Your crop, location, approximate site area, whether the site is new or existing, and your timing and objectives. A short description is enough to begin.

Can you give a fixed price at the first stage?

Costs depend on project scope and site conditions, so a proposal follows an initial technical review rather than preceding it.

Do you guarantee yields or energy output?

No. Outcomes depend on the site, crop, climate and how the system is operated. We assess options against your requirements and state our assumptions clearly.

Are regulatory approvals included?

Approvals are specific to each project. Responsibilities for them are set out in the proposal for that project.

How this is calculated
  • Marketable produce = area × gross yield per m² × (1 − wastage).
  • Revenue = marketable produce × price per kg.
  • Operating profit = revenue − annual operating costs.
  • Margin = operating profit ÷ revenue.
  • Break-even price = annual operating costs ÷ marketable produce.
  • Simple payback = capital cost ÷ annual operating profit. It is shown as not reached when profit is zero or negative.
  • The scenarios apply your percentage changes to yield, price and operating costs.

The calculator does not include financing, taxes, depreciation, seasonal variation or the timing of cash flows. It does not use market data. Your figures stay in your browser.
